# Rubric for the written explanations

PRD §7.1 says that after the last sitting, each tester answers three questions in writing, in a few sentences, without the game open. Two team members score each answer as **correct**, **partly correct** or **wrong**, and only correct answers count towards the target. This rubric is fixed before the playtest (8 Dec 2026) so that the scoring doesn't drift once the answers are in.

The team must approve this draft before the playtest. After that it doesn't change.

## How to score

1. **Score each answer on its own,** without the tester's code, quiz result or other answers in view.
2. **Two scorers score every answer independently** and write down a score and a short reason for each.
3. **Disagreements are settled together** with this rubric, and the agreed score and the reason are written down. If you still can't agree, the lower score counts.
4. **Content counts, wording doesn't.** Spelling, grammar, length and game terms don't matter. "Fritid", "free time" and "the evenings I had left" are the same thing.
5. **Use what the tester actually played.** If the tester describes something that really happened in their own game and the mechanism is right, the answer is correct, even when it isn't one of the examples below. Check it against the records (FR-50) if you have to.
6. **A wrong mechanism outweighs a right one.** An answer that gives the right cause but also a clearly wrong one (for example "the game decided at random") is partly correct at most.
7. **Report the agreement too:** the share of answers both scorers scored the same before talking. This shows how reliable the rubric is.

## SM-1: An earlier choice and a later result (target 70%)

**Question (Norwegian, as the tester sees it):**
> Tenk på én ting du valgte tidlig i spillet. Hva skjedde senere på grunn av det valget, og hvorfor?

| Score | What the answer must contain |
|---|---|
| **Correct** | (a) A specific earlier choice, **and** (b) a specific later result in money or wellbeing, **and** (c) the link between them: how the choice led to the result. |
| **Partly correct** | Two of the three. For example, a choice and a result without any link ("I bought a moped, and later I had less money"), or a vague link ("everything got worse because of it"). |
| **Wrong** | No specific choice or no later result, a result with no link to an earlier choice, a link the game doesn't have, or "don't know". |

**Examples of correct answers:**
- "I said yes to extra shifts in the autumn. That gave me more money, but fewer free evenings, so my social life went down and I got more stressed."
- "I put the pengegave in BSU. When I was 18 I had more saved because of the interest and the tax deduction, so I reached the savings goal."
- "I used the credit card on a phone. The bill came the month after and I couldn't pay all of it, so I paid interest and got a crisis month."

**Note:** "later" means a later month. A result in the same moment the tester chose doesn't count as a later result.

## SM-3: The wellbeing score and the ending (target 60%)

**Question:**
> Hva viser trivselen i spillet? Hvorfor fikk du den slutten du fikk?

The answer has two parts, and both must be right for it to be **correct**.

**Part A: what wellbeing shows.** It must say that wellbeing is about how the player feels or is doing, not about money, **and** name at least two of the three parts: stress, free time and social life (FR-33).

**Part B: why the ending came out as it did.** It must link the tester's own ending to both (1) whether the life goal was reached **and** (2) whether wellbeing was high enough at the end (FR-41). A tester who reached the goal with low wellbeing should say it was a hollow win because wellbeing was too low. For a loss, it is enough to name the money problem (the unpaid bills or debt at 20).

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Correct** | Part A **and** part B are right. |
| **Partly correct** | One of the two parts is right. Or both parts are there, but the ending is explained by money alone or by wellbeing alone when both mattered. |
| **Wrong** | Neither part is right, wellbeing is described as money or points for doing well, or "don't know". |

**Example of a correct answer:**
- "It shows how you're doing: stress, free time and friends. I saved enough for the flat, but I worked a lot and was stressed, so my wellbeing was too low and I got a hollow win."

**Note:** the tester doesn't need to give the threshold (50) or the weights.

## SM-4: More work hours, fewer activity slots (target 60%)

**Question:**
> Hvorfor fikk du færre aktiviteter (kvelder) i månedsplanen når du jobbet flere timer?

| Score | What the answer must contain |
|---|---|
| **Correct** | The chain: more work hours → less free time → fewer activity slots (FR-52). It must say that free time is what the hours take from, or that the slots come from the time left after school and work. |
| **Partly correct** | It names only one end of the chain ("because I worked more"), or describes the effect without the reason ("you just get fewer slots"), or gives the right chain and also a wrong cause. |
| **Wrong** | The answer says the slots depend on money, on the month or on chance, or "don't know". |

**Example of a correct answer:**
- "Work takes time, so there was less free time left. Fewer hours free means fewer evenings for activities."

**Note:** the tester doesn't need to say that the change starts the next month, or give the formula (free time ÷ 20).

## Before the playtest

- **Test the rubric:** two team members score 5-6 made-up answers, a mix of correct, partly correct and wrong. Where they disagree, make the rubric clearer before the playtest.
- **Check the questions:** check the Norwegian questions with someone outside the team who is 15-20. The questions must not give away the answer, for example by naming stress or free time in SM-3.
- **Store the answers by tester code,** never by name or email address (§7.1, NFR-3). Delete them together with the other playtest data after the analysis.
