- (note by coach) Research: comparables split into two camps - life-sims (BitLife: narrative but shallow/gamey finance, criticized for desensitizing to financial recklessness) vs fin-lit apps (Zogo, Greenlight, EVERFI, Gimi: correct-ish finance content but quiz/courseware, no narrative agency). No competitor pairs deterministic finance engine + LLM narrative.
- (note by coach) Nordic comparables: Gimi (Sweden, ages 7-15, pocket-money+gamified lessons, backed by Nordea/Tink, 800k+ downloads) is nearest existing product but skews younger and is courseware not simulation. Nordea Okonomipeil and Finans Norge (Skolemeny.no, NM i Pengequiz) are bank-school fin-lit programs, quiz/courseware. Finans Norge's own 2025 research says Norwegian youth struggle with financial knowledge and schools underuse the potential - validates the problem. No Norwegian product combines simulation + deterministic finance + AI narrative - LifeMode would be first-mover domestically.
- (decision by coach) Differentiator (for brief): deterministic financial engine + LLM-generated branching life narrative + Nordic life-stage script (confirmation money, russ, Lanekassen, husleiekontrakt, BSU) + B2B2C bank/school channel proven by EVERFI/Zogo/Okonomipeil but never attached to a simulation game before
- (gap by coach) Skeptic risk to address: is Claude-as-GM necessary vs cheaper static courseware (Zogo/EVERFI prove bank distribution works without an LLM)? Two specific LLM-GM failure modes to design against: (1) helpfulness bias letting players dodge consequences, undermining the core pitch; (2) content-safety failure with an all-minors user base (AI Dungeon 2021 precedent) plus GDPR/minors-data exposure in Norway/EU. Unit economics: per-session LLM cost is nontrivial vs teen/family subscription price point; needs human content review for a minors product.
- (event by user) User chose coaching path - working through differentiation/risk framing together before drafting
- (decision by user) Why AI (differentiator, hardened through coaching): (1) narrative/dilemmas vary combinatorially with accumulated state so no two playthroughs or players converge on the same 'game-story'; (2) open-ended natural-language negotiation with NPCs (e.g. arguing with the bank for a lower interest rate) where argument quality matters - something a static decision tree cannot do
- (decision by user) Guardrail on AI negotiation: the deterministic financial engine sets a base value and a bounded interval (e.g. a rate range); Claude/AI can only select within that interval based on argument quality, never outside it - preserves 'Claude creates the life, the financial engine determines the consequences'. Team explicitly does not want the AI to be lenient/too nice - outcomes must stay realistic.
