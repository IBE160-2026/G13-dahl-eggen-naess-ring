# User Research

## Pre-development User Research

Before development, two separate surveys will be used to validate the underlying problem, user needs and product assumptions.

---

### Player Survey

The player survey will explore:
- whether the problem feels relevant
- which financial situations are most interesting
- whether the concept sounds entertaining rather than like traditional schoolwork
- whether mechanics such as Life Balance and persistent consequences make sense
- - whether users would be interested in playing such a game

### Parent Survey

A separate parent survey will investigate:
- whether parents see value in giving teenagers a safe environment for practising financial decisions
- what functionality they would expect
- which concerns they have around privacy and AI
- whether there is willingness to pay for a future Family version

---

## Research Purpose

The **player** survey should help establish:
- whether the financial situations feel relevant to the target group
- which situations and decisions are most interesting
- whether the concept sounds engaging
- whether the proposed game mechanics make sense

The **parent** survey should help establish:
- which functionality parents consider necessary before they would consider paying
- the main concerns parents identify around privacy and AI

These surveys validate the problem, user needs and commercial assumptions, while later user testing evaluates whether the finished solution actually delivers the intended experience.

---

## Research Approach

The initial surveys will be anonymous and distributed online.

The surveys will combine structured questions, such as multiple-choice and rating-scale questions, with open-ended questions. Structured questions will provide quantitative results, while open-ended responses will provide qualitative insight into users' motivations, expectations and concerns.

Additional qualitative research, such as concept testing or interviews, may be used if deeper insight is needed.

## Results: Player survey «Unge og penger» (22-29 October 2026)

Anonymous Google Form, 133 responses. Raw data is kept outside the repo; only aggregated results and short, non-identifying quotes are recorded here. Percentages are of all 133 respondents. Analysed 2026-10-31.

**What the survey covered, and what it did not.** It measured young people's money habits, knowledge and wishes. It did not ask whether the LifeMode concept sounds fun, or whether mechanics such as wellbeing and lasting consequences make sense. Those questions from the Research Purpose above are still open. The team decided on 2026-10-31 not to run the parent survey in this round.

### Respondents

| Age | n | Share |
|---|---|---|
| 15-16 | 50 | 38% |
| 17-18 | 75 | 56% |
| 19-20 | 8 | 6% |

The 19-20 group is too small for conclusions on its own.

**Where their money comes from** (multiple choice): parents or pocket money 68%, part-time job 52%, stipend or student loan 41%, summer or odd jobs 36%, gifts 36%, selling things (Finn, Tise) 24%.

**What they have used themselves** (multiple choice): bank account and card 98%, savings account 87%, own fixed costs such as mobile and transport 38%, borrowed from friends or family 35%, BSU 14%, buy now pay later 6%, credit card or consumer loan 4%.

### Habits and attitudes

- **Saving:** 73% save most or some of what they get; 26% spend most or all.
- **The trip from the brief** (trip costs NOK 3,200, you have NOK 1,500): work extra 29%, borrow from parents or friends 26%, stay home 20%, use savings 17%. Only one person chose buy now pay later or a credit card. Several free-text answers describe asking parents first.
- **Unexpected expense in the last year:** 36% had one that was hard to pay. About half of them (17% of all) got help from family.
- **Agree (fully or partly) with:**

| Statement | Agree |
|---|---|
| I know roughly how much money I have right now | 88% |
| I'd like to learn more about money | 63% |
| I feel ready to make my own money choices at 18 | 64% |
| I often run out of money before I get more | 39% |
| Before I buy something, I think about how it affects my money months from now | 38% |
| I buy things I regret later | 37% |
| I feel pressure from friends to spend money | 14% |

### Understanding of terms

| Term | Understand well | A little | Not at all |
|---|---|---|---|
| Budget | 86% | 13% | 1% |
| Fixed costs | 85% | 12% | 3% |
| Buy now, pay later | 71% | 26% | 4% |
| Frikort / tax card | 63% | 26% | 11% |

BSU is understood well by only 26% of 15-16-year-olds (57% of 17-18-year-olds). Asked what happens if you buy a NOK 5,000 phone on a credit card and pay only the minimum each month, 72% answered correctly (you pay more than NOK 5,000); 28% answered wrong or "don't know".

**Where they learned most about money** (up to three): parents or family 88%, social media 42%, school 38%, own mistakes 32%, bank or bank apps 15%, friends 11%.

### Free text: what they regret buying

Recurring themes, roughly by frequency:

1. **Small impulse purchases that add up:** snacks, food and drink they did not need, takeaway. «Jeg kjøper smågodt veldig ofte og pleier å angre ved slutten av uken når jeg ser hvor mye penger det blir til sammen.»
2. **Clothes** they did not use or like, often bought online or without trying them on.
3. **Trends and social comparison:** a jacket that was trending on TikTok, an expensive laptop «bare på grunn av at jeg følte alle hadde en».
4. **Collectibles and chance-based purchases:** blind boxes, Pokémon cards and in-game purchases. One describes getting hooked after a lucky draw.
5. **Commitments they did not understand:** a gym membership never used, and a binding period on a subscription they were not aware of.
6. **Running out before the month ends:** «jeg hadde ikke penger til andre ting, som feks mat i kantina, eller faste utgifter.»
7. **BSU seen as a trap by one respondent:** the money is locked, and NOK 300,000 is «ikke nok til leilighet engang».

### Free text: what they wish someone had taught them

Most frequent: budgeting and saving; interest; BSU, credit cards and loans; paying bills and invoices; funds, stocks and investing (mentioned often, including by 15-16-year-olds); housing loans and moving out; available stipends. Several ask for it to be explained more simply and taught earlier in school.

### What this means for LifeMode

- **Supports the wellbeing differentiator.** The regrets are about value for money and satisfaction, not about the amount. That fits the idea that good finance is about how money is used, not just how much you end up with (see `research/playtest-competitors-2026-10-31/notes.md`).
- **Thinking ahead is the weak spot.** Only 38% consider how a purchase affects their money months later, and 39% often run out. That is exactly what persistent consequences and the choice → consequence link (#19, theme 2) should train.
- **Family is the main source of money, knowledge and help.** Borrowing from or asking parents is a common real-world choice, both in the trip dilemma and with unexpected expenses. The event library should include family as an actor, with its own consequences.
- **Event library candidates:** small purchases that add up, trend-driven buys, collectibles and loot-box-style spending, in-game purchases, subscriptions with binding periods, unused memberships, takeaway.
- **Teaching priorities:** BSU and credit/consumer loans are the least understood terms, and BSU is weakest among 15-16-year-olds. Explanations must use plain language. The BSU disclosure should cover both the NOK 0 deduction without tax and the fact that the money is locked.
- **Investing stays out for now.** Funds and stocks are a frequent wish, but investing is not one of the five money mechanisms in the brief. Decided on 2026-10-31: kept out of version one for now; a candidate for later.
- **Current use of credit is low** (4%), and buy now pay later is rare (6%). The credit mechanics from 18 introduce something most players have not tried yet, so they need more explanation than the rest.

### Limitations

- A convenience sample, probably spread through the team's own networks and schools, so it is not representative.
- Self-reported behaviour; for example, 14% reporting peer pressure may be low, given how many regrets in the free text are about trends and what others have.
- The 19-20 group has only 8 respondents.
- No questions on the LifeMode concept itself. No parent survey in this round (team decision, 2026-10-31).

## Later User Testing

Pre-development surveys can indicate whether the problem and concept appear relevant, but stronger validation requires users to interact with an actual prototype or MVP.

Once a playable version exists, user testing can evaluate whether:

- players understand the core gameplay and financial consequences
- the experience feels more like a game than traditional schoolwork
- users would voluntarily return for another session or playthrough
- the proposed mechanics work as intended
- users find the product valuable enough to use voluntarily
