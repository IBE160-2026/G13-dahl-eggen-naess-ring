LifeMode PRD — Validation Report

.docs/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-IBE160-G13-2026-10-22/prd.md (+ addendum.md, Rule Set v1)
Fair

This is a strong, unusually testable PRD. One protagonist carries six journeys, nearly every FR has numbered consequences tied to the worked-example answer key, and the glossary and IDs are clean enough for UX and architecture to extract from directly. The risk is in delivering it, not in defining it. The tier-and-date scheme added on 2026-11-13 (§4.4) contradicts itself, and as written it leaves the Extension tier unbuilt in the expected case. Meanwhile 46 of 57 FRs stay Core, and nothing says what happens if the core isn't playable before the 8 Dec playtest. A few stale figures, and architecture notes in the addendum that are only half superseded, should be cleaned up before the architecture run extracts them.

The three extra reviewers sharpen that picture rather than overturn it.

Engine arithmetic is sound. The independent figure check reproduced all 28 worked examples in Rule Set v1 with no mismatches, about 210 values in all. That includes examples 19–23, which so far only Claude's script had checked. It also found two real defects. FR-29 and the UJ-4 trace still use the old moped price. And a consumer loan in month 56 lets careless runs reach goal 3, despite "Borrowing never helps".

Bård's feedback is largely handled. The feedback-coverage review found 21 of 36 points applied and verified, 7 partly applied, and only one not addressed: OQ-11 hasn't been taken to the lecturer. Every change the memlog claims is present in the documents.

The adversarial review is the harshest. It finds that §3.1 states rules 5 and 8 as "or" where they mean "the larger of". Built as written, that puts UJ-4's Markus into crisis at once. It also finds that the ending summary can't show the spending level and working hours, which are the two levers that decide the ending; that UJ-4 works only because it leaves out fuel that Markus's own story says he pays; and that the card minimum of NOK 5,508 depends on a rounding rule that is never written down.

Where reviewers agree. Several reviewers reached the same conclusions independently: the §4.4 cut rules contradict each other (3 of 4 reviewers); FR-29 and the UJ-4 trace have stale moped values (3); moped fuel has a figure that the examples say doesn't exist (all 4); the §0 status block is stale (3); and Core FRs point at Extension or Can-be-cut features with no fallback (3).

Decision-readiness

adequate

Most decisions are stated as decisions, and the trade-offs are honest. The addendum's decision tables have "Why" and "Alternative rejected" columns and name what was given up. The §8 table is genuinely open: every question has an owner and a Blocks column, and none blocks UX.

The weak points are in the newest material. The cut rules added to §4.4 on 2026-11-13 contradict each other. The status block at the top of §0 describes the document's history rather than its current state. One game-design question that matters for the thesis is filed as a UX detail.

high

The §4.4 cut rules contradict each other, and on plan the Extension tier is never built

§4.4 "When to cut", checkpoint table, "Tiers"
The "When to cut" paragraph applies the first six cut items at the first checkpoint. The table drops only unstarted Extension FRs there, and drops the whole Can-be-cut tier at the second checkpoint. Extensions freeze about 26–27 Nov, but they're built only "once the core is playable", which is about 1–3 Dec. So all eight Extension FRs (avatar, recap, tips, consumer loans, visible opportunities, echo badges, disclosures, change password) drop in the planned case. Sprint planning will read these three rules three different ways.
Fix: Keep one rule set, written as a decision table: checkpoint → the condition tested → the exact consequence. Either move the Extension freeze after core-playable, or say plainly that Extension is expected to drop. Delete or adopt the "first six items" example, and say whether the 1–11 order still matters.

Strategic coherence

strong

The thesis is stated, and the features follow from it: players make expensive mistakes cheaply, the game always shows why, and time is as limited as money. SM-1, SM-3 and SM-4 measure understanding through written explanations scored against a rubric, not through self-report. Five counter-metrics guard the obvious failure modes. The weak joints are where cuts meet the journeys, and in two metrics.

Adversarial review (28 findings, unranked)

review-adversarial-general.md

This PRD is meticulous about arithmetic and edge cases. That is exactly why its gaps are dangerous: readers will trust it. The Core tier is nearly the whole product, and its own sequencing makes the Extension tier impossible to start. The §3.1 summaries misstate two rules in a way that would put UJ-4's Markus into crisis on day one. The flagship UJ-4 trace works only because it leaves out costs its own story says Markus pays. The ending summary, the payoff of "the game always shows why", can't show the two settings that the worked examples prove decide the outcome. A downstream team building from this document will either guess or stall.

AG-01

The Extension tier can never start under the PRD's own sequencing

§4.4 Tiers, dates table
The freeze (26–27 Nov) comes before core-playable (1–3 Dec). The avatar, the recap (all of UJ-2), tips, consumer loans, echo badges and disclosures all drop silently at the freeze.
Fix: Decide FR by FR: promote to Core, or move to Can be cut with a fallback. Or move the freeze after Core.
AG-02

Core isn't a minimal version, and the schedule isn't achievable for a student team

§4.4; NFR-1–11; FR-17; content check
About 18–20 days to core-playable, with UX, architecture and epics not yet written. The NFRs have no tiers. At least 40 events and all ending texts must be written in reviewed Norwegian. No checkpoint covers content authoring.
Fix: Define a "Core-0" walking skeleton for about 24 Nov. Tier the NFRs. Schedule content authoring and the harness with owners and weekly counts.
AG-03

Core FRs depend on non-Core FRs, with no "if not built" behaviour

FR-28→16, FR-42→19, FR-18/45/48/55/NFR-7→25, FR-12/49/55→57, FR-53→37
If FR-25 is dropped, FR-55 still obliges the engine to send a month-49 consumer-loan offer for a product that doesn't exist.
Fix: Add an "If not built" block to every Extension and Can-be-cut FR, listing the Core consequences that change.
AG-04

The rule for when to cut contradicts the checkpoint table, and nothing covers a Core miss

§4.4
Nothing stops Can-be-cut work, such as the driving game, from being built before Core. Dropping a tier that's already half-built saves no time.
Fix: Use one table. Add "No Can-be-cut work starts before Core is playable", plus a Core-miss rule.
AG-05

The cut order protects login hardening ahead of the game itself

Per-network limits, email caps and log-out-everywhere are cut last, for a closed playtest of 10–20 invited testers.

AG-06
