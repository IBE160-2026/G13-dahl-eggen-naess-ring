# c2 — Brettspill

**Idea.** LifeMode is a modern euro-style board game lying on a felt table. The five years are a printed saffron board: 60 squares in five serpentine rows, one row per age, with a checkered "20" finish medallion hanging off the edge. Markus is a chunky coral pawn. Money is brass coins and banknotes, choices are cards, and wellbeing is a set of score tracks with wooden markers.

## Game dynamics
- **Monthly loop = one move.** The hub's main button doesn't say "Continue". It says **"Flytt til april"**, and the pawn steps one square along the year strip. Quiet squares are plain cardstock, so a quiet month really is one tap. Coral squares carry a card icon and mean you **draw a highlight card**. Blue key squares are age unlocks (mopedlappen at 13, card and licence at 37). A hazard-striped tile shows that a crisis can turn a quiet square active.
- **Month plan = player mat.** School and work are hatched blocks printed on the mat and locked. You tap activity tiles into numbered slot spaces. Extra shifts physically cover slot 4 with a work block ("Tilbake i juni"). If you can't afford a tile, it greys out and shows "Mangler 160 kr".
- **Events = drawn cards.** Options read as moves A/B/C, each with a coin cost, a word ("fra brukskonto"), and token deltas. The free option is labelled "Gratis".
- **Consequences = pieces moving.** Coins slide off your stack and the stress cube steps along its track. The **echo badge** is a round blue token stamped "M14", linked by a dotted line back to the February square. **"Hvorfor?"** opens as a rulebook page with a ledger of causes. "Utsatt: mopedlappen" shows what the choice closed. The room is a player board where belongings are layers, and missing ones are printed outlines, like the dashed "mopedplass" waiting for a moped.
- **Wellbeing** uses three shapes as well as three colours (cube = stress, disc = fritid, hexagon = sosialt), always with numbers and the "Ekte seier: 50+" flag.

## Visual language
Teal felt ground with grain, saffron cardboard with a speckle pattern, cardstock cards, and drop-shadowed depth with a visible tile thickness. Type is Bricolage Grotesque for display (box-lid character) and Barlow Semi Condensed for tabular UI. Motion intent: one authored moment, the pawn hop plus coins sliding when you move.

## Why it works for 15–20
A board game is social, physical and readable at a glance. Progress is literally visible (row 2 of 5), and the metaphor makes "every choice carries forward" spatial instead of preachy.

## Risks
The screens are dense, and texture and depth may cost performance on cheap phones. The pawn on small squares can hide labels. Saffron-on-saffron panels need careful contrast for secondary text. The look could read as "family game" if the illustrations drift toward cute.

## Prototype first
The hub → "Flytt til april" pawn hop → event card flip → result with the echo token flying back to its source square. Test it on the bus with a sceptical 15-year-old.
