# c8 — Linjekart

**The idea.** Five years of life drawn as a transit line diagram. The 60 months are stations on Hovedlinja. Highlight events are interchange stations. Age unlocks are new lines that branch off: Mopedlinja from 16, Kortlinja from 18. The pengegave choice can put you on Sparelinja, which runs alongside the main line. The hub is "Neste stasjon", a departure board with your numbers. The year view is the line map itself, and the month plan is a ticket with seats.

**Game dynamics.** Each month you arrive at a station. A quiet month shows a quiet board ("STILLE MÅNED · ingen hendelse"), and you can leave in one tap ("Fortsett til april"). An active month is an interchange: the event screen draws a junction that splits into one coloured track per option. Each track ends at an option that states its kroner and wellbeing effect, so choosing is literally switching track. On the result screen the tracks you didn't take turn grey and yours carries on. An echo badge is a dashed loop line back to an earlier station ("Ekko fra februar: du lot jakka ligge…"). "Hvorfor?" sits right under the deltas. "Åpnet" and "Lukket" use transit grammar: a line that keeps going vs. a terminus bar. The month plan's seats make time visible: extra shifts hatch out seat 4 ("Tatt av ekstravakter"), and an activity you can't afford shows "Mangler 288 kr" instead of a place button. The room is a flat pictogram drawing with an empty dashed "LEDIG PLASS" where the moped will go, a goal you can see. Unlocks you haven't reached yet show as dashed future lines on the map, which acts as a teaser all the time.

**Visual language.** Swiss/international style on white. Strict 20px margins, bold flat line colours (cobalt, green, amber, magenta), black interchange rings, and one amber-on-black departure board as the "now" surface. Schibsted Grotesk (a Norwegian grotesk) does the talking. JetBrains Mono is used only for figures, months and board data. Nothing relies on colour alone: hatching marks crisis and missing money, dashes mark echoes and the future, and every delta has a sign and a word. Motion would come from the line: on Continue the station marker slides one tick along the line, and new branches draw themselves in.

**Why it works for 15–20.** It looks design-literate and grown-up, not childish or like a bank. Teens already read line maps every day on the bus, which is where they will play. "Five years, sixty stops" makes the whole game understandable in one image.

**Risks.** It can feel cool and austere, and it lacks warmth and humour. The room and the friends' messages have to carry the personality. Dense screens like the money view push the limits of the 390 px grid. The metaphor is weaker for crisis months, which are drawn as hatched red rings.

**Prototype first.** The junction → result transition: tap an option, the track lights up, the station marker moves, and the echo loop draws in. Test whether teens read "switching track" as consequence.
