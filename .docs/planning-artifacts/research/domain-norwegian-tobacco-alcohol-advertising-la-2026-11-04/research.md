# domain research: Norwegian tobacco and alcohol advertising law for a youth game

**Decision this research serves:** May LifeMode events at age 15-17 let the player accept or refuse tobacco or alcohol, with the car licence gift depending on keeping the deal, when the purpose is to show the negative sides of the products?

**Outcome:** on 2026-11-04 Dahl proposed leaving these events out of the game for now, and the team confirms on 2026-11-05. The report stands as the basis if the team returns to the question.

This report is not legal advice. Legal conclusions marked "inference" are the research's own reading. No regulator or court has said them.

## Executive summary

**The events are probably allowed, on conditions. The reason is that the game has no sales aim, not that it shows the negative side. Showing the negative side is also not proven to help.**

1. **Both bans are general, and both turn on marketing purpose.** They cover all ages, so the team's framing of a "ban on marketing to minors" is wrong. "Reklame" means "massekommunikasjon i markedsføringsøyemed" in both regimes [2][8]. No freestanding ban on depiction was found (inference, medium), and no exception fits education, prevention or games [1][2][8]. A brand-free game with no ties to the trade and no commercial aim therefore appears to fall outside (inference, medium confidence).
2. **A good purpose does not protect the game by itself.** Under the alcohol circular, and by analogy for tobacco, the assessment looks at what the sender's purpose must be presumed to be, and one sales aim is enough, however worthy the other aims are [9][10]. The protection lasts only while the facts hold: no brands, no industry link, no commercial motive and no promotion using the content.
3. **No binding age rule was found that stops the events (inference), but PEGI points to 16+.** PEGI is voluntary in Norway, and bildeprogramloven leaves out games [19][20][22]. If the game were ever rated, PEGI's own text says "always PEGI 16 or PEGI 18", which is above the youngest players [18].
4. **The effect on young players is unknown.** No study found tests this design. Negative film portrayals did not protect in the one longitudinal study on the question [29]. In a review of 8 serious games, 6 raised knowledge and only 1 reduced use [35].

**Biggest caveat:** no decision or guidance on games, fiction or prevention was found under either ban, and Markedsrådet's archive could not be searched directly. A written answer from Helsedirektoratet is the most direct way to reduce this uncertainty. It is responsible for interpreting the alcohol rules and has supervisory authority over both advertising bans [1][52].

**Recommendation:** the team can keep this design for the December delivery if it follows the conditions in section 7. Ask Helsedirektoratet in writing before real players under 18 get access. Making the gift random and leaving out these events remains the option with no exposure at all.

## 1. Tobacco law

**The ban.** Tobakksskadeloven § 22 says "Alle former for reklame for tobakksvarer er forbudt", with no reference to age, and it extends to surrogates, which include vapes [1]. The regulation defines advertising as "Massekommunikasjon i markedsføringsøyemed, herunder avbildninger av tobakksvarer" and more [2]. Helsedirektoratet's guidance makes purpose decisive: communication made "med formål om å fremme salget av produktet eller andre produkter/tjenester" is advertising [3].

**Depiction.** A full-text search of the Act found no hit for "avbild", "film", "spill" or "fremstill" [1]. In the regulation, depictions appear only under "herunder", so they are a subset of marketing communication and not a separate ban (inference, medium) [2]. Smoking situations count as indirect advertising only "i reklame for andre varer og tjenester" [2].

**Exceptions.** The regulation's exceptions are limited to trade and foreign-media cases, and none names art, education, prevention or games [2]. The guidance adds editorial coverage and private statements, and both fail if the trade is behind them or the private person gains a benefit [3]. So the game relies on the general purpose test, not on an exemption. The directorate can grant dispensations "i særskilte tilfeller" [2].

**What cuts the other way:**
- "Reklamebegrepet tolkes strengt og omfatter både direkte og indirekte reklame" [3].
- "Tobakksvarer må ikke inngå i reklame for andre varer eller tjenester" [1]. This could reach trailers, screenshots and social posts for the game (inference). Whether promoting a free game counts is untested.
- "Bruk av bestemte farger og layout/utforming som forbindes med bestemte tobakksvarer" is indirect advertising, with no "i reklame" qualifier in its text [2].
- The regulation exempts indirect advertising "av uvesentlig omfang" in foreign TV programmes and foreign-produced film, but has no equivalent for Norwegian productions [2]. The exemption's opening words suggest it presumes nothing about what is caught, so this argument looks weak (inference, medium). A journal commentary treats on-screen smoking as relevant to this exemption [6].

**Enforcement.** Helsedirektoratet supervises, can order correction and coercive fines, and appeals go to Markedsrådet. Violations carry fines [1][2].

**Changes under way.** The guidance is under revision, with completion expected around 1 January 2027 [4]. Prop. 97 L (2025-2026) is pending and would raise the § 44 penalty range. "Reklame" had no hits in the chapters read, but the full bill was not checked [5].

**Age limit.** § 17 forbids selling or handing over ("selge eller overlate") tobacco to under-18s [1][17]. It governs real transactions, and no rule keyed to a fictional character's age was found (inference, medium).

## 2. Alcohol law

**The ban.** Alkoholloven § 9-2 says "Reklame for alkoholholdig drikk er forbudt", with no reference to age [7][9]. Alkoholforskriften § 14-2 defines advertising as mass communication "i markedsføringsøyemed" and adds: "Det foreligger markedsføringsøyemed dersom et siktemål med kommunikasjonen er å fremme salg" [8]. It lists sender, initiative, content and design as factors [8][9]. The tobacco regulation has no such list, so applying these factors to tobacco is an analogy.

**How purpose is judged.**
- The test is "en helhetsvurdering av hva som må antas å være avsenders formål" [9]. A stated intention therefore does not settle it.
- Mixed purpose does not help: "Det avgjørende er om ett av formålene dekkes av begrepet markedsføringsøyemed, uavhengig av hvor akseptable og rettmessige de øvrige formålene er" [9]. The ministry's reading of Rt. 2000 s. 46 agrees [10].
- Economic interest is "en sterk indikasjon" of marketing purpose, typically for the trade, "men ikke privatpersoner og uavhengige medier" [9].
- Rt. 1995 s. 1176 supports the purpose test: a shop manager displaying wine was acquitted. The court took advertising to mean communication aimed at promoting sales, and the display was treated as a political statement, so the case is not a ruling on depiction. This is reported through the circular; the judgment itself was not read [9].

**Depiction and games.** Games appear only as a marketing channel ("ulike spill"), and game content is not discussed [9]. The circular treats images of people in drinking situations as alcohol-positive and a possible sign of sales purpose, in a context that appears commercial (medium) [9]. Paid product placement is covered, and unpaid depiction is not addressed [9].

**Exceptions.** None of the listed exceptions covers art, games or prevention. The closest on education, nr. 16 on courses and lectures closed to under-18s, does not fit. Another exempts sober, non-product-specific facts and illustrations in channels the recipient must actively seek out [8]. This could be a fallback for alcohol, with no tobacco equivalent (inference) [2].

**Enforcement.** Helsedirektoratet can fine businesses since 17 September 2024, but not private persons acting privately [11]. Breaches can also bring fines or up to six months in prison under § 10-1, or up to two years if especially serious [7]. Every case found has a commercial sender. In the closest, Ballinciaga, a music group was fined NOK 1.5 million. According to the directorate's news item, targeting children and young people was a main reason the breaches counted as serious; the decision itself was not read [12]. Supervision has focused on the trade, with growing concern about influencers [14]. Stortinget has reportedly asked for a full review of the ban, but adoption and follow-up are unconfirmed (low) [15].

**Age limits.** They bind the seller or server: "Derimot er det ikke straffbart for personer under de nevnte aldersgrenser å kjøpe eller drikke alkoholholdig drikk" [13]. Buying alcohol for an under-18 is forbidden [7]. Whether parents may give their own child alcohol at home is unresolved: one public answer glosses "utlevere" as "gi" [16], while the regulator frames the limits around "skjenking og salg" [13].

## 3. Children and age ratings

**PEGI.** The Drugs descriptor applies when a game "refers to or depicts the use of illegal drugs, alcohol or tobacco", and such games "are always PEGI 16 or PEGI 18" [18]. "Refers to" could also catch an offer the player refuses (inference). PEGI's published text has no educational exception and says nothing about player choice or negative consequences, but the assessment form was not read [18][19]. Non-glamorised use appears to land at 16 (inference, medium) [18].

**PEGI is not law.** The Code binds publishers who sign up to it [19]. The ministry calls it "den frivillige merkeordningen" and saw no reason to anchor it in Norwegian law [20]. Medietilsynet describes the age limits as recommended [21]. So PEGI would not stop a 15-year-old from playing.

**Other rules.**
- **Bildeprogramloven** does not mention games, and the legislator deliberately kept them out [20][22].
- **Markedsføringsloven §§ 19-21** apply to trade practices in consumer relations [23]. A free game can still be "levere en ytelse", so the deciding question is whether the operator is "næringsdrivende" (inference, medium). A sponsor, a company or real brands could change the answer.
- **AVMSD:** its recitals exclude primarily non-economic activity and name "on-line games" as outside, but only the recitals of the 2010 text were read (medium) [24].
- **DSA:** it regulates intermediary services [25], and a game serving only its own content is probably not one (unverified). Norwegian adoption could not be confirmed (low) [26].

**Personal data.** Datatilsynet's test for health data is broad. Citing a case on purchase records, it says such records can count if they reveal health directly or indirectly [27][28]. No guidance on in-game choices was found. A fictional character's choice does not on its face describe the real player (inference, low to medium). The risk rises if choices are tied to a named player and shown as real habits, or if the game asks about real use.

## 4. Prevention evidence

**The design is untested.** No experimental or longitudinal study of an in-game offer that a minor can accept or refuse was found.

**Negative framing does not remove the risk (low to moderate strength, film and smoking only).**
- In 6,522 US adolescents, character smoking predicted initiation "regardless of character type". The effect of negative portrayals was stronger among teens lower in sensation seeking. The data are observational, and the size of the effect was not retrieved (medium) [29].
- Among Scottish 19-year-olds, film smoking showed no association with smoking, which matters for the top of the age range [30].
- In Norwegian focus groups with 54 pupils aged 13-17, negative or anxious smoking characters drew weaker identification than elegant ones. Behaviour was not measured [32].
- An anti-smoking ad before a film offset the scenes' effect on views among about 800 ninth graders (medium) [33]. In an Australian cinema study it moved non-smokers by 4 points, had no significant effect on smokers, and may have raised intention among young women smokers (medium) [34].

**Games.**
- In 1,094 UK adolescents aged 11-17, playing at least one commercial game with such content was associated with trying smoking (aOR 2.70) and alcohol (aOR 2.35). The study is cross-sectional and says nothing about negative framing or cause (medium) [31].
- A 2014 review of 8 serious games found 6 that raised knowledge, 2 that raised negative attitudes, and only 1 that reduced frequency of use (medium) [35].
- A 2022 scoping review of 29 studies calls results generally positive for acceptability, usability and efficacy, but several study-level results are null or adverse, and it did not analyse mechanics systematically. In *One Shot*, the closest match, enjoyment mediated the effects of in-game risky decisions on intention to drink less and on refusal self-efficacy. In *Perfect Pour*, longer play was linked to more positive attitudes to binge drinking, while higher scores were linked to more negative ones. Effects faded in *Take Control* and the alcohol-only *LezParlay*. The review was read in part (medium) [36].

**Tone.** Helsedirektoratet's school resource says information "bør være nøytral og faktabasert" and lists stigmatising terms to avoid, including "misbruk" [37]. "Ikke skremsel" is a recurring theme in stakeholder input to its programme proposal, with a note that scare tactics work for some young people but not others, and it is unattributed [38]. The research on fear messages disagrees: one review, not limited to adolescents, found 8 of 17 studies favourable [39], EUDA rates the effect as unknown [40], and a SAMHSA summary calls scare tactics ineffective for youth [41]. Evidence for a boomerang caused by the message itself is weak.

**The licence deal.** No study or survey of it was found. Cochrane found no significant effect of the Smokefree Class Competition on starting smoking, on very low to low quality evidence. It reported no adverse effects, though one study suggested more false claims about smoking status in order to win [42]. A 2012 meta-analysis found a benefit for current smoking (RR 0.86), and the conflict is unresolved (low to medium) [54]. For Tobaksfri Duo, a contract with an adult gave a relative risk of 1.11 (CI 1.00-1.22) of staying cigarette-free, in a secondary analysis open to selection effects [43]. A public advice column calls such conditions "en motivator som ofte virker", with no data (exact page unconfirmed) [44].

**Many players already use.** Weekly or daily snus reaches about 23% of upper-secondary boys and 15% of girls, and vape about 7% and nearly 11% [45]. In 2025, 14% of lower-secondary pupils had been clearly drunk in the past year (medium) [46]. In Ungdata 2020, the latest upper-secondary figure read, 80% in VG3 had been drunk in the past year (stale) [47]. So claims like "hardly anyone drinks" would be false, and a moralising tone addresses many real users.

## 5. Cross-dimension insights

1. **The legal conditions and the prevention evidence point the same way.** No brands, no attractive images and no glamour should lower both the legal risk and the exposure that the film research warns about, so text-only events serve both (inference, untested).
2. **The protection depends on facts the project could change.** The brief's addendum says Family Premium is "already in the business model", and `market-validation.md` lists it as potential future revenue. Paid features tied to this content could give the sender an economic interest, which the guidance treats as a strong sign of marketing purpose [9]. Paid features could also make the operator "næringsdrivende" [23]. The legal position should be checked again before any commercial step.
3. **The project cannot test the effect on the age group that carries the risk.** Playtests only include 18-20-year-olds (PRD NFR-3), while the unknown risk sits with 15-17-year-olds. The December delivery exposes few or no minors, so the real decision comes at go-live.
4. **A rating would sit above the youngest players.** A game for ages 15-20 would probably carry PEGI 16 if rated (inference) [18]. This has no legal effect today, but the team should decide knowingly, since app stores or schools may ask later. Those rules were not researched.

## 6. Contrary evidence

A separate skeptic searched for reasons the conclusion is wrong. It found nothing fatal to the narrow legal outcome, but the original wording did not survive. It said showing the negative side "is not a marketing purpose" and that there was "no meaningful risk". This report replaces those with "appears to fall outside" and "unknown".

- **Negative framing and harm:** tobacco-company "prevention" ads aimed at parents were linked to lower perceived harm and more smoking among older teens. The data are observational and the sender was the industry, so transfer to a non-industry game is unproven (medium) [48].
- **Prevention can backfire:** 10 of 93 articles screened on school programmes reported iatrogenic effects, with the size often unclear (medium) [49]. A meta-analysis of 17 school-based brief alcohol interventions found improvements [53]. This evidence concerns programmes, not games.
- **The strongest legal objection:** the test is not what the author says the purpose is, the concept is read strictly, and no authority says negative framing takes content outside the ban. "Very likely outside on these facts" is defensible. "Does not breach, because negative content is not marketing" is not.
- **Public funding:** tobacco treaty guidance reportedly suggests making productions with tobacco imagery ineligible for public subsidies. The guideline text and Norway's commitments were not read (low) [50].
- **Not searched:** Markedsrådet's archive (unreachable), Forbrukertilsynet, Sivilombudet, Nordic and UK regulators, Actis, and any view on rewards for abstaining. These are gaps, not passes.
- **Counterpoint:** Helsedirektoratet's own school resource links a film on smoking and has pupils plan a campaign against tobacco [51]. This shows prevention material exists, but it does not state that such material falls outside the ban.

## 7. Recommendations

**For OQ-1 in the PRD (#34):**
1. **Keep the design as an allowed option, on the conditions below** (medium confidence, resting on inference from the definition). If the team wants no exposure, a random gift with no such events remains simple and safe.
2. **Correct the framing.** In OQ-1, UJ-4, the PRD addendum and the meeting prep, replace "ban on marketing to minors" with "general advertising ban". Basis: [1][7], high confidence.

**For NFR-8 and the event library (FR-17), as requirements:**
3. **No brands or look-alikes.** Use generic words like "snus" or "øl", with no real or invented product names, logos, flavours or pack design [2][8].
4. **No ties to the trade.** No funding, sponsors, ads, links to sellers or prizes from the industry [3][9].
5. **Text-only events, and no products in the room (FR-34).** No images of use or products [2][9]. The legal basis is medium confidence, and it also limits exposure.
6. **No tobacco or alcohol in promotion.** Keep these events out of trailers, screenshots and social posts for the game [1]. High confidence on the text, low on how it applies to a free game.
7. **No gain from accepting.** The PRD requires a wellbeing effect for every option (FR-13), so accepting must not give a bonus or in-game advantage [9]. This creates a tension the team should settle: real teens do drink for social reasons, and the tone must stay believable. Inference, untested in a game.

**For how the events are written:**
8. **Show the cost in kroner, not health horror.** Use the lost licence and the monthly price of snus, explained under "Hvorfor?". This fits "nøytral og faktabasert" [37] and what the game is about. The fit is inference, and the guidance itself is high confidence.
9. **No stigma.** Avoid "misbruk" and similar terms, and don't suggest that players who use are failures, because many are in that group [37][45].
10. **Keep the legal claims accurate.**
    - Don't say it's illegal for a minor to drink [13].
    - You can say it's forbidden to buy alcohol for an under-18, or to sell or hand over tobacco to one [1][7].
    - Don't say whether parents may give their child alcohol at home [13][16].
11. **No way to hide use and keep the gift.** The engine tracks the deal, and the game does not reward lying, given the false-claims signal in [42]. Low to medium confidence.

**For success metrics and privacy:**
12. **Don't claim the game reduces use.** Promise understanding and reflection, which is what the evidence can support [35][36].
13. **Don't ask about real use, and treat the choice as game state.** Don't show it as evidence of real habits, including in any later parent view. Add this to the privacy check (#24) [27][28]. Low to medium confidence.

**Before go-live to real players under 18:**
14. **Ask Helsedirektoratet in writing** whether these events fall outside both bans. Add this next to #24. This would settle more than further searching.
15. **Record the PEGI position in the PRD:** the PRD plans no rating, and a rating would probably be 16 (inference) [18][19].
