# Digest: red team of the legal and risk conclusion, assistant 1

All sources accessed 2026-11-04. Fetched = opened, but text via a summariser capping quotes at 125 characters. Snippet = search text only. 15 tool calls.

**Conclusion attacked:** a non-commercial, brand-free educational game can include these events without breaching the bans, because the bans cover marketing purpose only, showing negatives isn't a marketing purpose, and there is no meaningful risk of harm.

**Bottom line (skeptic):** nothing fatal; could not break the narrow legal outcome. Every enforcement example had a commercial sender. Three parts should not stand as written: "no meaningful risk" is categorical and unsupported; "showing the negative sides is not a marketing purpose" is an inference with no authority; the conclusion omits PEGI.

## Serious
- F1 PEGI label above the youngest players. Drugs descriptor applies when a game "refers to or depicts the use of illegal drugs, alcohol or tobacco"; always PEGI 16 or 18. No carve-out for educational or negative portrayal. "Refers to" could catch the refuse branch. Whether a rating is required for this game not established. https://www.pegi.info/what-do-the-labels-mean | high on wording, medium on application | fetched. Damage: serious.
- F2 Educational purpose doesn't settle § 9-2. "Det avgjørende er om ett av formålene dekkes av begrepet markedsføringsøyemed". "Helhetsvurdering" of sender, initiative, content, presentation, channel. Courses and lectures in principle covered. "Faglig" and "opplysning" not general exemptions. Images of people in drinking situations treated as alcohol-positive. Games listed as a carrying service. Negative or critical content not addressed. Same page treats economic interest as a strong indicator; this game has none. https://www.helsedirektoratet.no/rundskriv/alkoholloven/kapittel-9-reklameforbud/-9-2.reklame-for-alkoholholdig-drikk | 2016-02-19 | high | fetched. Damage: serious to the stated reason, minor to the outcome.
- F3 Wakefield et al: tobacco-company parent-targeted "prevention" ads linked to lower perceived harm, stronger approval, greater intention, more past-month smoking; strongest in grades 10 and 12. Observational; industry sender. https://ideas.repec.org/a/aph/ajpbhl/10.2105-ajph.2005.083352_9.html | AJPH | 2006; https://consumer.healthday.com/mental-health-information-25/addiction-news-6/big-tobacco-s-anti-smoking-ads-boost-teen-smoking-535826.html | medium | snippet. Damage: serious to the premise that negative framing protects; transfer unproven.
- F4 1,094 UK adolescents 11-17: aOR 2.70 smoking, 2.35 alcohol. Cross-sectional, commercial games. https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/34054 | 2016 | medium | snippet. Damage: serious to "no meaningful risk".
- F5 Systematic review screened 93 articles on school programmes; 10 reported iatrogenic effects, mostly on use; size often unclear. Meta-analysis of 17 school brief alcohol interventions found improvements (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25294110/). https://core.ac.uk/works/73325175 | date unconfirmed | medium | snippet. Damage: serious to arguing "no risk" from silence.

## Strongest good-faith argument (skeptic's construction, no expert retrieved)
- Health lawyer: the test isn't the author's stated purpose; one marketing purpose suffices; read strictly; no educational exception. "Very likely outside the ban on these facts" is defensible; "does not breach, because negative content is not marketing" isn't. Holds only while facts hold: no brand, no industry contact, no outside funding, no promotion showing the events.
- Prevention expert: exposure in games correlates with use; some negative messaging correlates with worse outcomes; prevention harm under-reported. An accept branch is untested: "unknown risk, needs evaluation".

No experimental or longitudinal study showing an educational game or negative in-game portrayal raised intention or use.
