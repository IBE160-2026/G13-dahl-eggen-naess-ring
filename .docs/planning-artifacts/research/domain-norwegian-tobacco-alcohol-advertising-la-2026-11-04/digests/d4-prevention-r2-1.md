# Digest: d4 prevention evidence, round 2, assistant 1

All sources accessed 2026-11-04. Read status: full, partial, abstract, excerpt. All reads via WebFetch's summarising model; check numbers and quotes against the original before reuse. 20 calls, 6 distinct sources read (four fetches failed).

## Bottom line (assistant)
- Negative framing is not shown to be protective. In the one longitudinal study, exposure to negative smoking characters still predicted initiation, more strongly among teens lower in sensation seeking.
- Q1 partly answered: study and direction established, effect size not.
- Evidence doesn't test the proposed format: passive film viewing, smoking only. No study of an in-game offer a minor character can accept or refuse.
- Q4 and Q5 largely open.
- Inference: supports "negative framing does not remove the risk" at low to moderate strength, for film and smoking. No conclusion either way on interactive refusal events.

## Q3. Martínez-Miranda and Espinosa-Curiel, JMIR Serious Games 10(3):e39086, Aug 2022
URL: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9459843/fullTextXML | partial (first 100,000 of 165,012 characters)
- C13 29 studies from 629 records, Jan 2010-May 2022, ages 12-30, prevention and treatment. 11 pilots, 10 RCTs, 5 quasi-experimental, 2 comparative, 1 proof-of-concept. "Generally positive" for acceptability, usability, efficacy. high
- C14 Mechanics not analysed systematically; no pooled choice-and-consequence result. Choices & Consequences (Elias-Lambert et al): player liking only. medium to high
- C15 One Shot (Hong et al): enjoyment mediated effects of game time and in-game risky decisions on intention to drink less and refusal self-efficacy. Closest to the proposed mechanic. medium
- C16 Nulls: Shadow (branching dialogues) no difference from non-gamified; CollegeLiVE; Pure Rush (drug cues paired with negative stimuli) no differences in engagement or intentions; Drug Defense larger knowledge effect, lower intent, no reduction in use. medium
- C17 Adverse or weak: Perfect Pour (Willmott et al) longer play linked to more positive attitudes toward binge drinking, higher scores to more negative; Shots (Boendermaker et al) motivation fell in gamified condition; Gamberini et al leaflets more informative. medium
- C18 No game with substance offers in the portion read. Refusal skills in Recovery Warrior 2.0, One Shot, Take Control. medium
- C19 Of 10 RCTs, 5 had follow-up, 3 at 2 months. Effects faded in Take Control and alcohol-only LezParlay. high
