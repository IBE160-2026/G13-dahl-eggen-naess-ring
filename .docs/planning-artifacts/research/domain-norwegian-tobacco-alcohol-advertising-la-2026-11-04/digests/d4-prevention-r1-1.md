# Digest: d4 prevention evidence, round 1, assistant 1

Accessed 2026-11-04 for every claim. Read status is excerpt unless marked full. "n/d" = no date retrieved.

## Bottom line (assistant)
- No study tests this design: a game where accepting tobacco or alcohol has negative consequences, measured against real use.
- Most likely result (inference): more knowledge and perhaps firmer attitudes, no proven effect on use.
- Backfire: not established. Evidence for a message-driven boomerang is weak.
- Main open risk: the depiction itself. Seeing smoking predicts uptake in observational film research; one excerpt suggests a negative portrayal may not cancel that. Needs checking first (lead 1).
- The licence deal: no evidence it works. Closest evidence is borderline or null.

## Q1. Fear and negative-consequence messages
- Esrick et al. reviewed 17 studies 2005-2017 (10 tobacco, 5 alcohol, 1 marijuana, 1 methamphetamine). 8 found fear messages more effective than positive-emotion or control messages on "substance use-related measures". Not adolescent-only; share measuring actual use unconfirmed. https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09687637.2018.1424115 | Drugs: Education, Prevention and Policy | 2018 | medium | systematic review
- EUDA rates overall effectiveness of this approach as unknown. https://www.euda.europa.eu/best-practice/evidence-summaries/scare-tactics-and-fear-based-messages-inform-choices-substance-use_en | EUDA | n/d | medium | agency summary
- SAMHSA CAPT summary of 1993-2014 research: scare tactics ineffective for youth. https://www.PreventionActionAlliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/fear-messages-prevention-efforts.pdf | SAMHSA CAPT (third-party host) | 2015 | medium | narrative summary
- Fear and fact-based education can raise knowledge and negative attitudes, not shown to reduce use. https://theathenaforum.org/sites/default/files/Ineffectiveness%20of%20Fear%20Appeals_0.pdf | Athena Forum | n/d | low | advocacy summary
- Boomerang, experimental: adolescents rated tobacco-industry-sponsored ads less favourably, intention to smoke unchanged. Industry ads only. https://tobaccocontrol.bmj.com/content/15/1/13.full.pdf | Tobacco Control | 2006 | medium | experiment
- Boomerang, qualitative: smokers reacted with anger, defiance, denial; non-smokers supported messages. College students. https://epublications.marquette.edu/comm_fac/361 (mapping inferred) | Wolburg | 2006 | low | qualitative
- "Dogmatic or authoritative" alcohol messages can boomerang, especially adolescents and young adults. Second-hand citation. https://acmelab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/priming_effect_of_antismoking_psas_on_smoking_behavior-_a_pilot_study.pdf | Harris, Pierce, Bargh | n/d | low | secondary

## Q2. Imagery exposure
- 1,094 UK adolescents 11-17: having played at least one game with alcohol or tobacco content linked to ever trying smoking (aOR 2.70) and drinking (aOR 2.35). Cross-sectional, commercial best-sellers; nothing on negative framing. https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/34054 | Cyberpsychology, Behavior and Social Networking | 2016 | medium for association, low for cause
- UK cohort at 15: top quartile film smoking exposure vs lowest, initiation RR 1.73 (95% CI 1.55-1.93). https://thorax.bmj.com/content/66/10/856 (mapping inferred) | Thorax | 2011 | medium | cohort
- Six European countries, longitudinal: film smoking predicts onset across cultures. Not traced to one URL. | 2018 | medium
- "Character smoking predicts adolescent smoking initiation regardless of the character of the smoker, but negative portrayals of smoking have their greatest impact on adolescents low in risk-taking." Direction unconfirmed. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2731111 or PMC2639243 (not mapped) | n/d | low until read
- Anti-smoking ad before a film reduced the film's pro-smoking effect (about 800 non-smoking 14-15-year-olds per excerpt; design unconfirmed). https://www.upi.com/Archives/1996/12/19/Teen-smoking-research/2530850971600/ | UPI | 1996 | low | press report
- WHO Smoke-Free Movies report reportedly says films with tobacco should carry an adult classification; reached only via commentary; no WHO statement on games found. https://issup.net/knowledge-share/resources/2016-12/smoking-films-adolescents-and-unregulated-exposure | ISSUP | 2016 | low | secondary

## Q3. Serious games
- 2014 review: 8 serious educational games. 6 increased knowledge, 2 increased negative attitudes, 1 reduced frequency of use. https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/dar.12102 | Rodriguez, Teesson, Newton; Drug and Alcohol Review | 2014 | medium | systematic review
- 2022 scoping review: 29 studies ages 12-30, 14 on alcohol, 11 pilots, 10 RCTs. Positive on acceptability, usability, efficacy; long-term unstudied; mixes prevention with treatment. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9459843/ | JMIR Serious Games | 2022 | medium | scoping review

## Q5. Contracts and rewards
- Cochrane incentives review: no significant effect of Smokefree Class Competition on starting smoking; very low to low quality. No adverse effects, but one study suggested more false claims about smoking status to win. https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD008645_do-incentives-help-keep-young-people-starting-smoke-medium-long-term | Cochrane | 2017 | medium | systematic review
- Meta-analysis of 5 studies: benefit for current smoking at follow-up (RR 0.86). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22285973 | Eur Addict Res | 2012 | low to medium
- Tobaksfri Duo (TOPAS), secondary as-treated, 586 students: 55% stable contract, 35% unstable, 10% none. RR of remaining cigarette-free end of grade 9: 1.11 (95% CI 1.00-1.22), any contract vs none. Selection likely (inference). https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/ses/resource/pt/mdl-37875648 | Prevention Science | 2023 | medium
- TOPAS protocol: earlier evaluations too weak for firm conclusions. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7661241 | JMIR Research Protocols | 2020 | medium
- ung.no answers: licence conditions common and "en motivator som ofte virker"; one case of a 16-year-old who broke the deal by getting drunk. No data. https://www.ung.no/oss/dWlW8OHfYqS1iaLP7uxYDb and siblings (mapping unconfirmed) | ung.no | n/d | low | Q&A
- Illegal for adults, including parents, to buy or give tobacco to under-18s; also illegal to sell, serve or give them alcohol. Not checked against Lovdata. https://www.ung.no/rusmidler/tobakk/4241_Aldersgrense_18_år_på_snus,_røyk_og_vape.html | ung.no | n/d | medium

## Q7. Refusal skills and normative feedback
- Review of 23 digital interventions on refusal self-efficacy: low-quality, encouraging, mainly girls. Outcome is self-efficacy. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10243864/ | Health Promotion International | 2022 | medium

## Design choices (assistant's inference, untested in a game)
1. Tone neutral and factual, believable consequences.
2. Depiction is the main risk until lead 1 checked; text events, no brands, no attractive imagery as cautious default.
3. Many players already use. Avoid moralising and stigmatising words.
4. "Most do not use regularly" is true per product; products can't be added. "Hardly anyone drinks" would be false.
5. Show the deal as one family's arrangement, not a proven method. Don't reward hiding use. Decide what happens after a breach.
6. Promise knowledge and attitude goals, not reduced use.

## Contradictions
- Fear messages: Esrick (8 of 17 favourable), EUDA (unknown), SAMHSA (ineffective). Different windows, outcomes.
- Smokefree Class: Cochrane null vs 2012 meta-analysis RR 0.86. Reason not established.
- Licence deal: ung.no "often works" with no data vs null incentive evidence.
- Refusal skills: encouraging digital review vs no mediation in school study.
- Cranwell: press says "directly influenced", design is cross-sectional. Excerpt "17 of 32 games (44%)" is inconsistent (17/32 = 53%); don't cite the share.
- Helsedirektoratet terms: search summary said avoid "rusmiddel"; the page read lists "misbruk", "narkoman", "rusmisbruker".
