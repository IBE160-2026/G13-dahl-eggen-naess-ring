# Domain research: LK20 personal finance curriculum

**Decision this research serves:** how LifeMode (a personal-finance simulation for players aged 15–20, so 10. trinn through VG3) should be positioned for school use, and what its 5-question before/after money quiz should test.

## Executive summary

**Lead with lower-secondary maths and samfunnsfag, then 2P and the vocational 1P-Y track. Present the game as a way to cover the cross-curricular theme *folkehelse og livsmestring*. It should not be presented as a stand-alone course.**

1. **The curriculum codes the brief named are out of date.** SAF01-04 and MAT01-05 expired on 31.07.2026. Their replacements, SAF01-05 and MAT01-06, have applied since 01.08.2026 [3][21]. Upper-secondary 1T has also moved, to MAT09-02 [6]. Materials for school year 2026/27 should cite the new codes.
2. **The closest fit is 10. trinn maths.** MAT01-06 now explicitly names *"renter på sparing, lån og kredittkjøp"* (interest on savings, loans and credit purchases) [2]. That covers LifeMode's savings and loan/credit mechanisms almost word for word, and the wording is more explicit than in MAT01-05 [18]. In upper secondary, 2P and every 1P-Y variant have an explicit aim on loans and credit cards [5][7]. Plain 1P and 1T have none [19][6].
3. **There is a real gap that LifeMode can fill.** Norway scored below the OECD average in its first PISA financial-literacy test (2022), with 22.8 % of students at a low level [9][10][11]. SIFO (2025) reports that the aims are vague, there is no set syllabus, and teaching is often deprioritised [12]. The government's white paper names *"renter, budsjett og sparing"* (interest, budget and saving) as example content [13].

**Biggest caveat:** no core-subject aim names tax, frikort, BSU or consumer rights. The game's tax and BSU content can only be tied to aims indirectly: through income and consumption in samfunnsfag [1], gross and net income in 2P [7], and interest on savings in maths [2].

## D2: National guidance and initiatives

- **PISA 2022 financial literacy (Norway's first participation).** Norwegian students scored below the OECD average, with 22.8 % at a low level and 10.5 % at a high level. Norway has one of the widest gaps between its high and low performers. 79 % of students say they learned about money in maths and 38 % in samfunnsfag. Most feel confident with digital payments but not with bank forms, account statements or purchase contracts. Few compare prices. Few know what *rentes rente* (compound interest) means [9]. The OECD average was 498, and Norway was not among the seven systems above it [10] (Norway's exact score was not retrieved). ScienceNorway confirms the findings and adds that how much schools focus on finance varies [11].
- **SIFO/OsloMet, 2025.** 18–20-year-olds have a "middels" (medium) level of knowledge. Many say they were never taught personal finance, even though it has been in the curriculum since 2020. Teachers describe the aims as vague, with no syllabus and no fixed hours. SIFO recommends *"Tydeliggjøre kompetansemålene i læreplanene og utvikle pensum"* (clarify the competence aims and develop a syllabus) [12].
- **Regjeringen (government).** Meld. St. 34 (2023–2024) §5.2.4, "Styrke kompetanse i personlig økonomi", says: *"De nye læreplanene er tydelige på at elevene skal lære om personlig økonomi både i matematikk og samfunnsfag"* (the curricula are clear that pupils learn personal finance in both maths and samfunnsfag). It gives *"renter, budsjett og sparing"* as example content. Udir has been tasked with a *kompetansepakke* (teacher competence package) for folkehelse og livsmestring [13]. Meld. St. 32 (2020–2021) promised a national strategy for financial understanding; this run could not confirm whether it was ever published [14].
- **Finans Norge** runs Skolemeny.no (free, non-commercial school material), a lower-secondary maths project task ("Kalle": budget, credit card, saving, insurance), the guide "På egne ben", the annual *NM i Pengequiz* for ungdomsskole classes (every February) and a pensions slide deck (2023) [15].

## D4: Proposed before/after quiz (5 questions)

***These are proposals derived from the aims, not research findings.*** Each question covers one mechanism and one named aim, is meant to take under a minute, and should be checked by a teacher before use. The Norwegian question text should be written by the team.

1. **Wages and frikort (SAF01-05, 10. trinn [1]).** *You earn 40 000 kr from a summer job this year and it is your only income. You have frikort. How much tax is deducted?* (a) 0 kr ✔ (b) about 10 % (c) 22 % (d) 50 %. *Why:* frikort means no tax on yearly income up to 100 000 kr [16]. The 50 % deduction only applies if you go over the amount registered with an employer, so (d) is a deliberate distractor.
2. **Saving with interest (MAT01-06, *renter på sparing* and *vekstfaktor* [2]).** *You put 10 000 kr in a savings account at 4 % interest per year and leave it for 2 years. How much is in the account?* (a) 10 400 (b) 10 800 (c) 10 816 ✔ (d) 14 000. *Why:* 10 000 · 1.04² = 10 816. The interest from year 1 also earns interest (compound interest), which PISA found few Norwegian 15-year-olds understand [9].
3. **Fixed costs and budget (MAT01-06, *utforskande arbeid … personleg økonomi* [2]; 1P-Y budget aim [5]).** *Your take-home pay is 18 000 kr a month. Your fixed costs are rent 8 000, phone 400, transport 600 and insurance 300. How much is left for food, fun and saving?* (a) 8 700 ✔ (b) 9 300 (c) 9 700 (d) 10 000. *Why:* 18 000 − 9 300 = 8 700.
4. **Credit card and consumer loan (MAT01-06, *lån og kredittkjøp* [2]; 2P/1P-Y loan and credit-card aim [5][7]).** *You buy a 10 000 kr phone on a credit card and pay only the minimum each month. Which statement is true?* (a) It costs 10 000 kr as long as you pay something every month (b) The total cost ends up higher than 10 000 kr because interest is charged on the unpaid balance ✔ (c) Interest is only charged if you miss a payment (d) A credit card is always cheaper than a consumer loan. *Why:* interest is charged on the balance you still owe.
5. **Unexpected expense in a crisis month (folkehelse og livsmestring, *handtere utfordringar … personleg økonomi* [8]; SAK01-01 [4]).** *Your laptop breaks and a new one costs 4 000 kr. This month you have 1 500 kr left after fixed costs. Which choice usually costs least in total?* (a) Pay with buffer savings you put aside earlier ✔ (b) Take a small consumer loan (c) Put it on a credit card and pay the minimum (d) "Buy now, pay later" and push the payment forward. *Why:* using your own savings costs no interest or fees. Options (b)–(d) all add a cost.

The before/after design measures recall within a single session (10–15 minutes). It does not measure lasting learning (see open questions).

## Cross-dimension insights

- The aims (D1) and the white paper (D2) agree on *interest, budget, saving* as core content [2][13]. Researchers also say the aims are too vague for teachers to plan from [12]. An aim-tagged game with a ready quiz addresses exactly that complaint: it gives teachers a concrete plan for a vague aim.
- Positioning should follow the school track. Academic VG1 (1P, 1T) has no loan or credit aim [19][6], so in VG1 the game fits **vocational 1P-Y** best. On the academic track it fits **2P (VG2)** and samfunnskunnskap.

## Recommendations

- **Positioning (brief and PRD):** lead with *10. trinn MAT01-06 + SAF01-05*, then *VG1 yrkesfag 1P-Y*, *VG2 2P* and *SAK01-01*, all framed under folkehelse og livsmestring. Confidence is high because these rest on verified aim texts. Cite the 2026 codes, not SAF01-04 or MAT01-05.
- **Teacher pitch:** point to the PISA gap [9] and SIFO's call for concrete material [12]. Confidence is high.
- **Content accuracy (PRD constraint):** take frikort and BSU figures from Skatteetaten [16][17] and re-check them every tax year. The BSU figures rest on a single source and are unverified.
- **Quiz:** use D4 as a first draft, labelled as aim-derived, and have it reviewed by a teacher.
